Using AI Tools Responsibly in Your Studies

How students can use AI tools to understand material, check their writing, and plan work, while following academic integrity rules and disclosing use where required.

Published 2026-10-24

AI tools can help students explain difficult concepts, test their understanding, and check the clarity of a draft. They can also produce confident-sounding text that is wrong, invents sources, or does the thinking the assignment is meant to develop. Using them well means knowing where the line is.

Rules come first

Check your institution's and instructor's policy before you use an AI tool on any assignment. Policies differ: some allow brainstorming, some allow grammar checks, and some prohibit AI entirely. When in doubt, ask in writing.

Useful ways to use AI

  • Explaining a concept in simpler language, then checking your understanding against your textbook
  • Generating practice questions on a topic you are studying
  • Asking for feedback on whether your argument is clear, while you make the changes yourself
  • Planning a study schedule

Risky uses

  • Asking the tool to write sections of an assignment you then submit as your own
  • Accepting citations without checking them. AI tools often invent references that do not exist.
  • Using AI-generated facts without verifying them in a primary source

Check every citation

If an AI tool suggests a source, find it in a library database or on the publisher's site. Confirm the authors, year, and title, then read the relevant part before you cite it.

Disclose where required

Some courses require you to state when and how you used AI. Keep notes on what you used and why, so you can describe it accurately if asked.

Your writing should show your thinking. If a tool does the thinking, the assignment has not done its job.
