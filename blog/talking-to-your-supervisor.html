How to Talk to Your Supervisor About Feedback

Practical advice for understanding supervisor comments, asking clarifying questions, and turning feedback into a clear revision plan for your thesis or project.

Published 2026-11-10

Supervisor feedback can be hard to read, especially when comments are brief or seem to contradict each other. Treating feedback as a conversation, rather than a verdict, makes it easier to use. Most supervisors want you to ask questions.

Before the meeting

Reread your draft and the comments. Sort the feedback into three groups:

  • Points you understand and agree with
  • Points you understand but disagree with
  • Points you do not understand

Bring that list. It shows your supervisor you have engaged with the feedback, and it makes the meeting efficient.

Ask specific questions

Vague questions get vague answers. Compare these:

  • Vague: "What did you think?"
  • Specific: "You wrote that my method section is unclear. Do you mean the sampling, the instruments, or the analysis?"

Disagree respectfully

You do not have to accept every suggestion. If you disagree, explain your reasoning and ask how your supervisor would handle the issue. Often the disagreement reveals an unclear sentence that caused the confusion.

After the meeting

Write a short summary of what you agreed to: the changes, the deadline, and any suggested reading. Send it by email if your supervisor is comfortable with that. This record prevents misunderstandings later.

Keep the relationship steady

Respond to feedback within the timeframe you agreed. If you will miss a deadline, say so early and propose a new date. Supervisors respond well to students who communicate clearly and make progress visible.
