A Proofreading Checklist Before You Submit

A final-pass checklist for students covering formatting, citations, numbers, headings, and file details, so small errors do not undermine a strong paper.

Published 2026-10-10

Proofreading is the final check, not the first. By the time you proofread, the argument should be settled and the structure should be final. Proofreading catches the small errors that readers notice first and remember longest.

Structure and layout

  • [ ] The title page, headings, and page numbers follow your style guide.
  • [ ] Headings are consistent in level and style.
  • [ ] Figures and tables are numbered in order and have captions.
  • [ ] Each figure or table is mentioned in the text.

Citations and references

  • [ ] Every in-text citation has a matching reference list entry.
  • [ ] Every reference list entry is cited in the text.
  • [ ] The reference list is in the correct order and uses hanging indents.
  • [ ] Author names, years, and titles match the original sources.

Numbers and facts

  • [ ] Numbers are consistent across the text, tables, and figures.
  • [ ] Percentages and totals add up.
  • [ ] Dates, names, and places are spelled correctly.
  • [ ] Abbreviations are defined on first use.

Language

  • [ ] Each sentence has a clear subject and verb.
  • [ ] Spelling and punctuation are consistent, including British or American spelling.
  • [ ] Words you have repeated many times are varied where it helps.

The file itself

  • [ ] You have submitted the file type and naming format requested.
  • [ ] The file opens correctly on a computer other than yours, if possible.
  • [ ] You have read the submission instructions one more time.

Read it aloud

Reading the paper aloud, or listening to a text-to-speech read-through, exposes missing words and awkward sentences that the eye skips. Do this as your very last step.
