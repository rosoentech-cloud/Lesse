How to Read a Journal Article Efficiently

A three-pass method for reading academic journal articles quickly, deciding which ones deserve a close read, and taking notes you can use in your own writing.

Published 2026-10-15

Academic articles are dense, and reading them from start to finish is slow. Most researchers read in passes. Each pass takes more time, and you stop once you have what you need.

Pass one: the overview (about 5 minutes)

Read the title, abstract, introduction's last paragraph, section headings, and conclusion. Answer three questions:

  • What question does this article ask?
  • What method did the authors use?
  • What did they find?

If the answer is not relevant to your work, move on. Saying no quickly is a useful skill.

Pass two: the main argument (about 20 minutes)

Read the introduction and discussion in full. Skim the methods and results, paying attention to tables and figures. Mark the claims that matter for your own argument, and note the evidence for each.

Pass three: the close read (as needed)

Read the full article carefully only for sources you will cite heavily or critique. Check the methods for weaknesses, such as small samples, missing controls, or unclear measures. Note where the authors overstate their findings.

Take notes you can use

For each article, record:

1. The full citation 2. The question, method, and key finding in one sentence each 3. The quotation or page number for anything you may cite directly 4. Your own reaction, including how the article relates to your argument

Writing your notes in your own words at this stage makes paraphrasing much easier later.
