Writing Your First Resume After Graduation

How to write a resume with little work experience, using coursework, projects, volunteering, and skills, with a structure that recruiters can scan in under a minute.

Published 2026-10-17

Recruiters often spend less than a minute on a first pass through a resume. For graduates with little full-time experience, that means the document must show relevant skills and results quickly. Coursework, projects, and volunteer work all count as evidence.

Choose a simple structure

1. Contact details. Name, city, phone, email, and a professional profile link if you have one. 2. Summary. Two lines on who you are and the role you want. 3. Education. Degree, university, expected or actual graduation date, and relevant honors. 4. Projects or experience. Coursework projects, internships, part-time jobs, or volunteering. 5. Skills. Software, languages, and tools, grouped by type.

Describe actions and results

Each bullet should say what you did and what happened as a result. "Analyzed survey data for a 200-person project using Excel pivot tables, which identified the two issues the team prioritized" is stronger than "Used Excel."

Match the job description

Read the posting and note the terms it uses for skills and responsibilities. Where your experience matches, use the same terms. Be truthful: include only skills you can discuss in an interview.

Keep it to one page

For most graduates, one page is the right length. Use a clean layout with consistent headings, a readable font, and enough white space that the page does not feel crowded.

Final checks

  • Remove any personal details you do not need, such as age or marital status, unless the local norms in your country expect them.
  • Save the file with your name in the filename.
  • Proofread for spelling and dates. Recruiters notice both.
