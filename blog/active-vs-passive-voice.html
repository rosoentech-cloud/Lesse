Active vs Passive Voice: When Each Works Best

Understand the difference between active and passive voice, why academic writing uses both, and how to choose the right form for clarity and accuracy.

Published 2026-11-01

In active voice, the subject performs the action: "The researchers collected the data." In passive voice, the subject receives the action: "The data were collected." Both are correct. Each suits different purposes, and many students overuse the passive without knowing why.

Why students use passive voice

Passive sentences often feel more formal or objective. Some academic fields, especially the sciences, traditionally use the passive in methods sections. That habit can spread into every part of a paper, where it makes sentences longer and harder to follow.

When active voice works better

Use active voice when the actor matters or when you want clear, direct sentences.

  • Weak: "It was decided that the sample would be reduced."
  • Clearer: "The team decided to reduce the sample."

When passive voice is the better choice

Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, unimportant, or obvious, or when the focus should stay on the object of the action.

  • "The samples were stored at minus 80 degrees." (The lab staff are not the point.)

How to check your draft

Search for "was," "were," "is," "are," "been," and "being" followed by a past participle, such as "was shown" or "were found." Each one is a candidate for active voice. Keep the passive when the reason is clear, and change it when it is not.

A note on fields

Check your discipline's guidelines and your instructor's preferences. Some journals specifically request active voice, while others accept the passive in methods sections. Clarity matters in every case.
