Your LinkedIn About section is the first thing many recruiters read after your headline. For students and recent graduates, it is a chance to explain your focus and direction without a long work history. A short, specific summary works better than a list of buzzwords.

## Structure that works

1. **Opening line.** Who you are and what you study or do, in one sentence.
2. **Focus.** Two or three sentences on the topics, methods, or problems you work on.
3. **Evidence.** A sentence or two on projects, coursework, or volunteering that show those skills.
4. **Goal.** What kind of role or collaboration you are looking for.

## Write in the first person

Use "I" and keep the tone warm and direct. A summary that sounds like a real person is more memorable than one written in corporate language.

## Keep it honest

Describe the skills you have, and mention the tools you have used in practice. Recruiters often check whether a skill appears in your projects and experience. Claims you cannot support will not hold up in an interview.

## Keep it current

Update the summary when you finish a project, start a new course, or change your goals. A summary that reflects what you are doing now is more useful than one written two years ago.

## Check the details

- Keep the summary under about 2,000 characters so it displays fully.
- Make sure your headline and summary tell the same story.
- Ask a mentor or career adviser to read it and tell you what they understand about your focus after one reading.
