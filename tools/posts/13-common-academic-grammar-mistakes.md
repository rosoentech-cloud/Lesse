Most grammar errors in academic writing are not random. They follow patterns, and once you know the patterns you can catch them quickly. Here are the errors that appear most often in student papers.

## Comma splices

A comma alone cannot join two complete sentences.

- Incorrect: "The sample was small, the results were inconclusive."
- Correct: "The sample was small, so the results were inconclusive." Or use a semicolon, or split the sentence in two.

## Subject-verb agreement

The verb must match its subject, even when other words come between them.

- Incorrect: "The effects of the treatment was measured twice."
- Correct: "The effects of the treatment were measured twice."

## Misplaced modifiers

A modifying phrase should sit next to the word it describes.

- Incorrect: "Using a survey, the data was collected from 300 students." (The data did not use a survey.)
- Correct: "Using a survey, we collected data from 300 students."

## Dangling "this" and "which"

"This" and "which" should refer clearly to a single idea. When the reference is unclear, name the idea.

- Unclear: "The team changed the schedule, which caused concern."
- Clearer: "The team changed the schedule, and this decision caused concern among volunteers."

## Its and it's

"It's" means "it is" or "it has." "Its" is the possessive.

## Apostrophes in plurals

Plurals do not take apostrophes. "Results" is correct. "Result's" is not, unless you mean possession.

## Check your own patterns

Keep a list of the errors your instructor marks most often. Search your draft for each one before you submit. A grammar checker can catch some of these, but it will miss meaning-based errors.
