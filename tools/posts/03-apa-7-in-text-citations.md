In-text citations tell your reader where an idea came from. In APA 7th edition, each in-text citation points to a full entry in your reference list. This guide covers the cases students ask about most often.

## One author

Use the author's last name and the year, separated by a comma.

- Parenthetical: (Okafor, 2021)
- Narrative: Okafor (2021) found that...

## Two authors

Use both names every time. Join them with "&" in parentheses and "and" in the text.

- Parenthetical: (Lee & Patel, 2020)
- Narrative: Lee and Patel (2020) argued that...

## Three or more authors

In APA 7, cite the first author followed by "et al." from the first citation onward.

- (Nguyen et al., 2019)

## Group authors

Use the organization's name, and an abbreviation in brackets only if you will use it again.

- First citation: (World Health Organization [WHO], 2022)
- Later citations: (WHO, 2022)

## Multiple sources in one citation

List them in alphabetical order by author, separated by semicolons.

- (Adams, 2018; Brown, 2020)

## Page numbers and direct quotes

Include a page number for direct quotations. Use "p." for one page and "pp." for a range.

- (Adams, 2018, p. 45)

## Common mistakes

- Citing a source you have not read, or only quoting it from another source without saying so.
- Forgetting that every in-text citation must match an entry in the reference list.
- Using "et al." for two authors. It applies only to three or more.

Check your style guide or your instructor's requirements, because some courses and journals set their own rules.
