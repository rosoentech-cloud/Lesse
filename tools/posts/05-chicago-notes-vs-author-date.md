The Chicago Manual of Style offers two citation systems. They present the same information in different ways, and each suits different fields. Using the wrong one is a common reason for formatting feedback, so check which one your course or journal requires before you start.

## Notes-bibliography

This system is common in history, the arts, and parts of the humanities. Sources are cited with numbered footnotes or endnotes. A full bibliography appears at the end.

- A note gives the full citation the first time a source appears, then a shortened form after that.
- The bibliography lists each source alphabetically by author's last name.

## Author-date

This system is common in the sciences and social sciences. Sources are cited in the text with the author's last name and year, similar to APA. A reference list appears at the end.

- In-text: (Smith 2020, 14)
- Reference list entry: Smith, Anna. 2020. *Title of the Book*. City: Publisher.

## How to choose

- Check your assignment brief or the journal's author guidelines first.
- If neither says, look at the sources your instructor or supervisor uses in their own work.
- Do not mix the two systems in one document.

## One practical tip

Whichever system you use, keep your source notes organized from the start. Converting a paper from one Chicago system to the other late in the process takes far longer than most students expect.
