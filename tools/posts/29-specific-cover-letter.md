A cover letter should show the hiring manager why you are a good fit for this particular job. Generic letters that could be sent to any employer rarely stand out. Specific letters, even short ones, show that you have read the posting and thought about the role.

## A simple structure

1. **Opening.** Name the role and the organization, and say in one sentence why you are applying.
2. **Evidence.** Choose two or three experiences that match the job's main requirements. Describe what you did and what resulted.
3. **Connection.** Explain why this organization in particular interests you, based on something specific you have read about its work.
4. **Close.** Thank the reader and state that you would welcome a conversation.

## Be specific

Vague: "I am a hard-working team player with strong communication skills."

Specific: "In my final-year project, I coordinated four students across three data sources and delivered a report that the partner organization used in its annual planning."

The specific version gives evidence the reader can picture.

## Keep it short

Three or four paragraphs is usually enough. Long letters are harder to read and tend to repeat the resume.

## Before you send

- Use the company's name and the role title exactly as the posting does.
- Check that the letter addresses the requirements you were asked to meet.
- Proofread for typos, especially in the employer's name. A wrong name is hard to recover from.
