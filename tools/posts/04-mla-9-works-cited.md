MLA 9 builds each Works Cited entry from a set of core elements, in a fixed order. You do not need to memorize a template for every source type. Learn the order, then fill in the elements that apply to your source.

## The core elements

1. **Author.** Last name first, then first name, as it appears on the source.
2. **Title of source.** Put titles of articles and web pages in quotation marks. Put titles of books in italics.
3. **Title of container.** The larger work that holds the source, such as a journal or website. Italicize it.
4. **Other contributors.** Editors, translators, or illustrators, if relevant.
5. **Version, number, and publisher.** For journals, the volume and issue. For books, the publisher.
6. **Publication date.**
7. **Location.** Page numbers for articles and books, or a URL for online sources.

## Examples

**Journal article**

Lee, Hana. "Remote Work and Team Trust." *Journal of Management Studies*, vol. 45, no. 2, 2021, pp. 110-128.

**Book**

Okonkwo, Ada. *Writing Across Disciplines*. Oxford University Press, 2019.

**Website**

"How to Cite a Website." *Writing Center*, 2023, www.example.edu/cite-website.

## Formatting the list

- Title the page "Works Cited."
- Alphabetize entries by the first word of each entry, usually the author's last name.
- Use a hanging indent, where the first line is flush left and the rest are indented.

## Check before you submit

Compare every entry with the source itself. Automatic citation tools often get capitalization, page numbers, and container titles wrong, so review them by hand.
