Most university assignments ask for peer-reviewed sources. Peer review means that experts in the field evaluated the article before publication. Finding these articles is easier when you know which tools to use and how to check what you have found.

## Start with your library database

Your library subscribes to databases such as EBSCO, ProQuest, JSTOR, or Web of Science. Use the advanced search, and filter by:

- **Peer-reviewed** or "scholarly journals" (the exact label varies by database)
- **Publication date** to keep the research current
- **Document type** set to journal articles

Searching by subject heading often finds more relevant articles than searching by keyword alone.

## Use Google Scholar carefully

Google Scholar covers a wide range of research. To make it useful, open Settings and link your library so that full-text access appears. Note that Google Scholar includes some material that is not peer reviewed, so check each source.

## Use the reference lists

Once you find a strong article, read its reference list. Look for authors and titles that appear more than once. Then search for those articles directly. This method, called citation chaining, often leads to the most important work in a field.

## Check whether a source is peer reviewed

- Look for the journal's name, and check the journal's website for its review policy.
- Be cautious with sources that have no named authors, no reference list, or no publication details.
- Check the publication date, and look for the article's DOI, a stable identifier that links to the published version.

## Record as you go

Save the citation, DOI, and a short note for each article in a reference manager or spreadsheet. Good records save hours when you write your citations later.
