---

description: How to end an essay or report with a conclusion that synthesizes your argument, explains its significance, and points toward what comes next.
category: Writing

---

A weak conclusion restates the introduction word for word, then stops. A strong one shows the reader what the argument has achieved and why it matters beyond the page.

## What a conclusion should do

- **Restate the thesis in new language.** Show what the argument now means after the evidence.
- **Summarize the key moves briefly.** Remind the reader of the main steps, without listing every point again.
- **Explain the significance.** Say what changes if the argument is accepted.
- **Point forward.** Suggest a limitation, a question for future research, or an implication for practice.

## What it should not do

- Introduce major new evidence or a new argument. Those belong in the body.
- Apologize for the paper's limits at length. Name a limitation once, precisely, and move on.
- Use phrases such as "In conclusion" as a crutch. A strong closing sentence signals the ending without announcing it.

## Example

Repetitive: "This essay has argued that tablets affect reading. Tablets are used in schools, and their effect on reading is important."

Stronger: "If the effect of tablets depends on teaching practice, then purchasing devices alone will not improve reading outcomes. Districts should invest in teacher training alongside hardware, and future studies should measure how lessons are structured, not only whether students have access."

## A quick check

Read your introduction and conclusion side by side. If a reader could swap one for the other without noticing, rewrite the conclusion so it reflects what your essay has shown.
